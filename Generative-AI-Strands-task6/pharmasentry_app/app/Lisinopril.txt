PRODUCT: Lisinopril (ACE inhibitor)

SECTION: indications_and_usage
Lisinopril is indicated for the treatment of hypertension, to lower blood
pressure. It is also indicated for the treatment of heart failure, as an
adjunct therapy, and for use in stable patients within 24 hours of acute
myocardial infarction.

SECTION: boxed_warning
When pregnancy is detected, discontinue lisinopril as soon as possible.
Drugs that act on the renin-angiotensin system can cause injury and death
to the developing fetus.

SECTION: warnings
Angioedema of the face, extremities, lips, tongue, glottis, and/or larynx
has been reported in patients treated with ACE inhibitors, including
lisinopril, at any point during treatment. Lisinopril should be discontinued
immediately and the patient monitored if angioedema occurs. Head and neck
angioedema may cause airway obstruction.
Excessive hypotension has been reported, particularly after the first dose,
in volume- or salt-depleted patients.

SECTION: contraindications
Lisinopril is contraindicated in patients with a history of angioedema
related to previous ACE inhibitor treatment, and in patients with hereditary
or idiopathic angioedema. Do not co-administer with aliskiren in patients
with diabetes.

SECTION: adverse_reactions
The most commonly reported adverse reactions in clinical trials were
dizziness, headache, cough, and hypotension. Cough was more common than
with comparator drugs and is a class effect of ACE inhibitors.

SECTION: drug_interactions
Concomitant use with potassium-sparing diuretics, potassium supplements, or
salt substitutes containing potassium may lead to increases in serum
potassium. Dual blockade of the renin-angiotensin system by combining
lisinopril with an angiotensin receptor blocker or aliskiren is not
recommended.

SECTION: dosage_and_administration
For hypertension in adults not on a diuretic, the recommended starting dose
is 10 mg once daily, titrated according to blood pressure response, up to a
usual maintenance range as determined by the prescriber. This information is
for label-reference purposes only and is not a substitute for a clinician's
personalized dosing decision.
